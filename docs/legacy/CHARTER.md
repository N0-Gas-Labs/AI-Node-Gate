# CTO Operating Charter (legacy)

> **Status: historical memory.** This is the operating charter of the organization
> that preceded this hub (`No-Gas-Labs-Official`). It is preserved because the rules
> it encodes outlived the repositories they governed. It is *not* a claim of live
> systems, and it contains no credentials or private keys.

The charter was the constitution of the prior portfolio: the small set of rules that
decided what deserved to exist, what was allowed to ship, and how the organization
would tell the difference between an experiment and a product. It is carried forward
almost verbatim, because most of it is simply good practice under any roof.

## Working principles

1. **One official home per product.** Many experiments may exist; only one is current.
   A product with two homes has none.
2. **No silent destruction.** Nothing is deleted or archived without a snapshot and a
   recorded decision. History is an asset, not a liability.
3. **Plain English first.** Every active repository must explain what it does, who it
   helps, its status, and its success criteria — in language a non-coder can read.
4. **Small releases over giant rewrites.** Visible, testable improvements beat more
   speculative projects. A small thing that works outranks a large thing that might.
5. **Money-bearing software is high risk.** Blockchain, payments, trading, and financial
   claims require controlled testing and independent review before any real-value use.
6. **Security before publicity.** No credentials, no unreviewed payment flows, and no
   claims that exceed the evidence.
7. **Every project needs an owner decision.** If a project has no current purpose, it is
   an archive candidate, not an active product.

## Status vocabulary

Every repository was required to carry exactly one of these labels, so that its
condition was legible at a glance:

- **Active** — being improved toward a defined user outcome.
- **Production candidate** — usable in a controlled demo, not yet proven for broad
  public use.
- **Prototype** — a working experiment used to learn.
- **Specification** — a written design that may not yet have working software.
- **Archive** — preserved history; no new feature work.
- **Duplicate** — a copy or alternate whose value is historical or comparative.
- **Security hold** — not approved for real money, sensitive data, or public claims
  until reviewed.

## Year-one operating rule (prior)

Active work was limited to four lanes: **Sovereign Forge / Oracle**, the **Podcast
Operations Suite**, **Games and Social**, and **EvidenceOS / Portfolio Intelligence**.
Finance and blockchain were permitted only as controlled research under **security
hold**. A new repository required a written reason and a named relationship to one of
the four lanes — a gate against breadth for its own sake.

## Definition of done

A release was not done because files existed. It was done when a person could use the
intended experience, the main path had been tested, the repository explained how to run
it, secrets were protected, and the owner could describe the result in one sentence.

## Decision gates

The charter drew a line between what a team could do on its own authority and what
required the owner at the gate. The team could, without asking: write documentation,
add tests, build local prototypes, and make reversible commits. It had to pause for the
owner before: deleting or archiving anything on GitHub, connecting real payments or
wallets, spending money, publishing new financial claims, or changing a flagship lane.

That line is the direct ancestor of this hub's founding rule: **autonomous nodes
propose; a human decides at the gate; every decision is signed and recorded.**
